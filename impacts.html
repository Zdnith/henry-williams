Impacts to spreading the Gospel:

Henry Williams played a key role in helping Christianity spread across Aotearoa. He did this by reforming how the missionaries worked in the CMS (Church Missionary Society), and helped them become more self-sufficient in spreading the word to more people. Williams focused on converting Māori directly first instead of civilising, so he did more local preaching and learning Te Reo Māori. This led to over three-thousand Māori baptisms and Christianity expanding past its original foothold because of him.

He required other missionaries to learn Te Reo Māori. Williams as well preached peace through a Christian lens, effectively helping Maori to stray away from 'utu', a cycle of revenge and resolved disputes among Māori.

Williams was also involved in translating a few religious texts in Māori alongside his brother, William Williams, and as well as the Treaty of Waitangi. However, he was given one night to translate the treaty and failed to convey the meaning of sovereignty to the British clearly, which caused many issues down the line that bleed into modern day.
